## 分节 1

Introduction: The project explores the necessity of implementing anti-addiction measures for college students, drawing parallels with existing policies for minors. With the pervasiveness of technology and internet, gaming has become integral to college students' lives, raising concerns about potential addiction similar to those observed in younger demographics. The study aims to understand the impact of gaming on college students and the efficacy of policies that limit gaming time, as successfully implemented for minors.
Method: Utilizing a survey method, the research collected data through questionnaires to gauge college students' perceptions and behaviors towards online gaming. The questionnaires were designed to capture quantitative and qualitative insights, including attitudes, gaming habits, and the influence of gaming on academic and social aspects of students' lives. This method allowed for a broad assessment while maintaining the flexibility to address specific research questions.
Result: The findings indicate that nearly half of the college students have a neutral view of online games, with a significant portion recognizing the benefits of gaming. However, a notable percentage of students are drawn to the strong appeal of games and use them as a means to relax. The survey also revealed that factors such as lack of self-control and family dynamics contribute to gaming addiction, suggesting a need for policies that enhance self-discipline and consider external support systems.
Conclusion: The project concludes that the core reason for college students' addiction to games is not the absence of external compulsion but their own self-management deficiencies. While appropriate gaming can provide relaxation and joy, mandatory policy controls may not be as effective for college students who have developed individual personalities and thinking. The research suggests that instead of restrictive policies, a focus on higher education and guidance for self-management could be more beneficial in preventing game addiction among college students. However, the study acknowledges its limitations, including a small survey scope, and recommends expanding the research to a more diverse and representative sample of college students.
