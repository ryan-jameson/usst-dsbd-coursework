## 分节 4

Abstract
In the context of heart failure, a leading cause of hospitalization in the United States,
approximately 15% of patients discharged within 30 days face readmission, contributing
to escalated healthcare costs and compromised clinical outcomes. This study leverages a
wealth of personal information extracted from Emergency Health Records (EHRs) spanning
two decades. This research aims to enhance the predictive capabilities of re-hospitalization
for cardiac patients by creating a two-stage LSTM model while incorporating features into
regression and time series datasets. A critical aspect of this study involves ensuring the
integration of all relevant features across both datasets. Including constant data elements,
such as demographics and cohort-level statistics, alongside time series data in the model
is deemed suboptimal. Consequently, the time series model accommodates two distinct
feature sets—one dedicated to time series information and the other to constant data.
This work demonstrates that the utilization of time series modeling significantly improves
predictive outcomes. Leveraging Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in the form of an
LSTM model, the research emphasizes the superiority of this approach in enhancing rehospitalization prediction accuracy for cardiac patients.

Chapter 1

Introduction
1.1

Overview

The challenge of unplanned rehospitalization of heart failure patients within 30 days of
discharge is a multifaceted issue in contemporary healthcare, demanding a nuanced and
comprehensive approach. Heart failure, a chronic condition impairing the heart’s ability to
pump blood effectively, is a leading cause of hospitalization among adults, particularly the
elderly. The recurrent nature of heart failure exacerbates the complexity of managing this
condition, leading to frequent hospital readmissions, which are both costly and detrimental
to patient quality of life [3].
The economic impact of heart failure readmissions is staggering, with the costs projected
to rise significantly in the coming years. This upward trend not only strains healthcare
budgets but also highlights inefficiencies in patient care and post-discharge management
[4]. The high rate of readmissions for heart failure patients indicates a pressing need for
improved strategies in managing this condition, both within hospitals and in transitional
care settings.
Advancements in medical care have improved the survival rates of heart failure patients,
yet the challenge of preventing readmissions remains. This paradox reflects the complexity
of heart failure as a medical condition, encompassing a wide range of contributing factors
including comorbidities, patient adherence to treatment regimens, and the effectiveness of
transitional care programs [5].

The integration of Big Data and Machine Learning in healthcare is revolutionizing
the field, enabling more accurate diagnoses, personalized treatment plans, and predictive
modeling for disease management. Big Data provides a vast repository of patient information, collected from various sources like Electronic Health Records, wearable devices,

technologies are particularly pivotal in managing chronic diseases, improving preventive
care, and tailoring treatments to individual patient needs.
The introduction of Electronic Health Records (EHR) has opened new avenues for
large-scale, data-driven approaches to tackle the issue of heart failure readmissions. EHRs
contain a wealth of patient information, including demographic data, clinical histories,
treatment records, and follow-up information. By leveraging this data, researchers and
healthcare providers can gain deeper insights into the patterns and predictors of readmission, enabling the development of more effective intervention strategies [6].
Despite the potential of EHR data, the current literature reveals a gap in utilizing this
resource effectively. Many studies have not fully capitalized on the richness of EHR data,
particularly in terms of temporal trends and detailed clinical information. Additionally,
there is a lack of consistency in the methodologies and variables used across different
studies, limiting the generalizability and applicability of their findings [7].
This research stands at the forefront of applying advanced machine learning techniques
to the pressing issue of heart failure readmissions. Through its innovative approach and
comprehensive data analysis, the study aims to contribute significantly to the field, offering
new insights and tools for healthcare providers to better manage heart failure patients and
reduce the frequency of unplanned readmissions.

Background
2.1

Introduction

The burden of heart failure (HF) and its associated economic impact, specifically in the
context of readmissions in the United States, forms the core of recent healthcare research.
As of 2013, HF affected 5.7 million Americans, costing $30.7 billion annually [8]. Readmission rates, particularly for patients aged 65 and above, have been high, with 1 in 4 HF
patients readmitted within 30 days of discharge [9]. This alarming trend led the Center
for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) to penalize hospitals with excessive readmissions,
marking a shift towards value-based care [10].
Predictive analytics in healthcare, especially for HF management, promises to control
rising hospitalization costs and improve outcomes. Big data solutions now allow for effectively managing large volumes of healthcare data. However, leveraging these for predictive
models in healthcare is challenging due to the noisy and heterogeneous nature of healthcare data. The use of machine learning and data mining techniques has shown promise in
overcoming these challenges, allowing for the identification of highly predictive variables
that traditional methods may have overlooked.
Despite the potential, the literature reports only modest performances of predictive
models for HF patient readmissions, with few models demonstrating an area under the
curve (AUC) of ± 0.70 [11,12]. Recent studies have been testing machine learning methods
for their potential to demonstrate higher performance. However, results show only little

In conducting the literature review for this study, the primary focus was on seminal research and foundational studies that have made substantial contributions to the field of
predicting heart failure readmissions. Specifically, papers utilizing methodologies involving
LSTM, Regression models, or incorporating relevant feature sets were carefully included in

and approaches envisaged in this study. Additionally, some papers explored the significance

were not included, as they often deviated from the core research objectives of this study.
The study performed by Padhukasahasram et al. [13] in 2015 focuses on predicting
unplanned readmissions and deaths after heart failure hospitalizations. It emphasizes the
significance of integrating clinical, behavioral, and socio-economic data for more accurate
predictions. The research used various models, including Cox proportional hazards and
random survival forests, on data from 789 heart failure patients. The key finding was that
models incorporating a comprehensive range of variables significantly outperformed those
using only clinical or behavioral data alone, highlighting the value of a holistic approach
in predicting heart failure readmissions.
The study performed by Zheng et al. [14] focuses on predicting hospital readmissions
using data mining and metaheuristic methods. The research explores various models,
including Radial Basis Function Neural Networks, Random Forest, and Particle Swarm
Optimization-based Support Vector Machines, applied to patient data. The key findings
reveal that the Particle Swarm Optimization-based SVM model, particularly with radial
basis function, significantly outperforms other models in predicting readmissions. This
model demonstrates high accuracy and sensitivity, making it especially useful for identifying
high-risk patients and improving healthcare resource allocation.

Dataset
3.1

Overview

A cohort of 8,802 individuals with encounter diagnoses associated with a set of ICD-9 codes
based on heart failure were gathered from a set of 16,216 unplanned ER admissions that
occurred between January 1, 2000, and January 1, 2020.
Patients who were pregnant or under the age of 21, those who left the hospital against
medical advice, those who were moved from another hospital, those without any temporal
features that could be identified, and those who passed away during the stay were not
included in the study. Furthermore, because they had much shorter average stays and
represented a different patient distribution, we did not include patients who were treated
exclusively in the intensive care unit. Even in cases where heart failure was not the primary
diagnosis, an encounter had to be linked to an ICD-9 code related to heart failure, as defined
by the eMERGE Heart Failure Phenotype [23], for it to be included in our dataset. For
our readmission prediction analysis, we considered every interaction a patient had.
In this particular patient group, there was a 6.0% death rate (or 981 encounters) and
an 8.5% positive readmission rate (or 1,393 encounters). The majority of the patient
population is White. The distribution of the patient race is shown in Table 3.1

– Associated with Encounters, it records specific ICD-9/ICD-10 diagnoses associated with the patient in a given encounter.

is also on enhancing data integrity and usability. This involves transforming irregular
time-stamped data into a structured format, suitable for analysis. Additionally, outlier

prepares the data for more accurate and insightful analytical modeling, setting a solid
foundation for subsequent predictive analyses. For features recorded multiple times, an
averaging method is applied to maintain consistency. Crucially, data with varying units,
particularly in medication doses, undergoes standardization to a uniform unit, followed by a
normalization process to facilitate comparability across different scales and measurements.

LSTM Model

Long Short Term Memory (LSTM), a variant of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), is
specifically designed to address the challenges of learning long-term dependencies. The
unique structure of LSTM cells includes three types of gates: Forget Gate, Input Gate, and
Output Gate. These gates collectively enable the network to retain or forget information,
making LSTMs adept at handling sequences with varying gaps and lengths in the input
data. The gates work by selectively allowing information to pass through, which helps
in maintaining the flow of relevant information throughout the sequence of layers, and
mitigating the vanishing gradient problem often encountered in standard RNNs. Fig 4.1
depicts the working of an LSTM Unit.

LSTM model architecture

The neural network architecture showcased here integrates both temporal and standard
feedforward processing layers. The LSTM layer is crucial for handling sequences, and effectively retaining information over longer periods, which is essential for time-dependent
data analysis. The input to the LSTM layer is the time-series features consisting of Lab
Results, Vitals, and Medication in a 72-hour format. The output of the LSTM is reshaped,
preparing it for subsequent processing through linear layers, which are standard building
blocks in neural networks. These layers are interleaved with ReLU activations, a common
choice for introducing non-linearity and aiding in complex function approximations. The
second input tensor consists of the Non static features which are Demographics and a few
engineered features discussed in Section 3.4. The inclusion of a concatenation step is to
combine the time series features with the non time series features, integrating diverse data
representations. A dropout layer follows, providing a regularizing effect that combats overfitting by randomly omitting subset features during training. The architecture culminates
in a linear layer that outputs a single value, indicative of a regression-oriented task, such
as predicting a continuous variable. This sophisticated hybrid design is tailored to extract

LSTM - Regression architecture

In predictive modeling, especially with Electronic Health Records (EHR), understanding
the temporal dynamics of patient data is crucial. The LSTM-Regression framework is designed to leverage the strengths of LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) models in capturing
time-dependent patterns before the data is used for regression analysis. This subsection
comprehensively explains how LSTM layers are utilized to process time series features, thus
enhancing the regression model’s predictive power.
After the LSTM layer has processed the time series data, the output is a refined representation of the patient’s historical data. This output is then used as input for the regression
model. The regression model, whether it’s a simple linear regression or a more complex
algorithm like Random Forest, can then make predictions based on this LSTM-processed
data.
The advantage of this approach is that it combines LSTM’s skill in capturing dynamic temporal relationships with the regression model’s ability to map these features to
outcomes. For instance, in predicting hospital readmissions, the LSTM layer can detect
patterns in a patient’s vitals over time, which the regression model can then associate with
readmission likelihood.

The model achieved reasonable AUC scores with demographic, vital signs, and medication
data. However, the addition of lab features substantially improved performance. The
most remarkable results were obtained by including cohort statistics as well, surpassing
the best-performing Regression model.
Furthermore, the LSTM-Reg model’s performance was evaluated under different time
step configurations. The model showcased competitive performance with a 1-hour time
bin, although it has yet to surpass the top-performing regression model.
Overall, this study highlights the importance of feature selection, comprehensive data,
and hyperparameter tuning in predictive modeling for patient outcomes. It underscores
the potential of deep learning techniques, such as LSTM, in leveraging diverse patient
data sources to enhance predictive accuracy. Further research and optimization are recommended to unlock the full potential of these models in clinical applications.
