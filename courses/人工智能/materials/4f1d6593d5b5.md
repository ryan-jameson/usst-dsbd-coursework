# Data Quality Assessment

### Imbalance Severity
- **Imbalance Ratio**: The dataset exhibits a high imbalance ratio of **10.5:1**, indicating that negative cases are significantly more prevalent than positive cases.
- **Positive Rate**: At a mere **8.7%**, the positive class represents a small fraction of the dataset.

### Modality Coverage
- **Data Completeness**: All **1689** entries possess clinical text, ECG data, and chest X-ray images, suggesting comprehensive multimodal input availability. This multimodality could enhance predictive capabilities if managed correctly.
- **Source Diversity**: The data is sourced from **MIMIC-III (1390 cases)** and **SuppCSV (299 cases)**, ensuring variability but may introduce source bias especially with significant reliance on one primary source.

### Risks
- **Prediction Bias**: High class imbalance could lead to a model biased towards the negative class, resulting in a risk of false negatives which is critical in a medical context.
- **Overfitting**: A model trained on imbalanced datasets may overfit to the majority class, failing to generalize on minority instances.

# Sampling Strategy Recommendation

### Oversampling Technique
- **Recommended Technique**: Use **SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique)**, as it generates synthetic examples of the minority class, allowing the model to learn a more generalized representation without merely duplicating existing samples.
  
### Oversampling Ratio
- **Target Ratio**: Aim for a **1:1 ratio** (or as close as possible) of positive to negative samples. This would require generating approximately **1400 synthetic positive samples**, balancing the dataset to **1542 positive** and **1542 negative** instances.

# Loss Function Recommendation

### Loss Function
- **Recommended Loss Function**: **Focal Loss** is suitable for imbalanced datasets as it focuses training on hard-to-classify examples while down-weighting easy samples.
  
### Hyperparameters
- **Gamma**: Set to **2.0** - this value enhances focus on rare classes while still allowing the model to learn from the majority class.
- **Alpha**: Set to **0.25** - this weight will mitigate the impact of the negative class on the loss function, increasing the importance of the minority class during training.

# Training Risk Warnings

### Potential Issues
- **Divergence in Loss**: If the model continues to predict all-negative, loss divergence could lead to unstable training, especially with adverse class imbalance.
- **Overfitting**: Training on synthetic positives without careful validation might lead to overfitting, where the model does well on training data but poorly on unseen data.

### Mitigation Strategies
- **Cross-Validation**: Implement **k-fold cross-validation** to ensure the model captures generalization capability across different data splits.
- **Early Stopping**: Utilize early stopping criteria based on validation loss to prevent overfitting.
- **Regularization Techniques**: Incorporate dropout layers in deep learning models (especially for ECG and image modalities) to prevent overfitting during training.
- **Ensemble Methods**: Consider using ensemble approaches like Random Forests or Gradient Boosted Trees which can handle class imbalance more gracefully. 

By systematically implementing these recommendations, we can improve the model's performance while addressing the inherent challenges of the imbalanced dataset.

### Conclusion

#### Experiment Overview
In Experiment 4, we aimed to enhance the predictive performance of our multi-modal medical AI model by implementing a series of robust strategies addressing the limitations observed in Experiment 3. Key motivations included improving AUC and F1 scores, particularly in a challenging dataset characterized by significant class imbalance (8.7% positive cases). We focused on various techniques, including model architecture adjustments, loss function modifications, sampling strategies, and data enhancement methods.

#### Key Results
The enhancements led to significant improvements in predictive metrics, showcasing that the Concat_v2 model outperformed the HyCF-TFM_v2 model, achieving a peak AUC of **0.952** and an F1 score of **0.511** compared to HyCF-TFM_v2's AUC of **0.890** and F1 score of **0.316**. Notably, Concat_v2 recorded an average precision (AP) of **0.656** and recall of **0.552**, demonstrating a comprehensive uplift in classification capabilities.

In absolute terms, the Concat_v2 model improved the AUC by **0.072** over HyCF-TFM_v2, equivalent to a **8.10% increase**. The introduction of new techniques, such as Focal Loss and the 1D-ResNet architecture, not only stabilized training but also effectively calibrated the model's ability to differentiate between classes.

#### Most Impactful Changes
Several changes had pronounced impacts:

1. **WeightedRandomSampler**: This intervention rectified the class imbalance, allowing for a more balanced training set and better representation of minority classes.
2. **1D-ResNet Architecture**: Replacing BiLSTM with a 1D-ResNet architecture notably enhanced the model's ability to process ECG data, yielding key improvements in AUC.
3. **Focal Loss**: This adjustment managed to focus on hard-to-classify samples, further improving the AUC and precision metrics.

#### Remaining Challenges
Despite these advancements, challenges remain. The HyCF-TFM model's F1 score of **0.316** and recall of **0.207** suggest persistent difficulties in accurately identifying positive samples. Additionally, the reliance on auxiliary losses and the full interdependencies among modalities could complicate model interpretability and real-world applicability. More advanced strategies are needed to boost the recall rate substantially for clinical relevance.

#### Clinical Relevance
The improvements observed in AUC (from **<0.08** in Experiment 3 to **0.952**) and the relatively high precision indicate that the models are more adept at making clinically pertinent predictions. This is particularly crucial in medical settings, where accurate identification of positive cases can lead to timely interventions. The balanced consideration of both false positives and false negatives underpins our models' readiness for preliminary clinical utility.

#### Next Steps
For Experiment 5, we recommend the following courses of action:

1. **Refining Class Balancing**: Investigating alternate sampling methods or synthetic data generation techniques (e.g., SMOTE).
2. **Ensemble Learning**: Combining predictions from both HyCF-TFM and Concat models to potentially leverage strengths of each.
3. **Hyperparameter Optimization**: Conducting a systematic grid search or Bayesian optimization to further fine-tune the learning rate and architectural parameters.
4. **Model Explainability**: Incorporating techniques such as SHAP or LIME to better interpret model predictions which is vital for clinical acceptance.
5. **Expanded Dataset Training**: Engaging in data augmentation with expanded datasets to bolster model generalizability and resilience against overfitting.

By focusing on these areas, we aim to substantially enhance the predictive capabilities of our model, ultimately paving the way for its integration into clinical workflows.
