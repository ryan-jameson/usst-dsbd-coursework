A Unified End-to-End Discrete Semantic Communication Framework
for Single-User Links and Equal-Power MAC
Yiqiao Zhou , Jianxin Ren, Na Wang, Xiaolong Hong, Zhiqian Meng, and Shuo Shao

Abstract—End-to-end (E2E) semantic communication aims
to transmit task-relevant information rather than bit-level fidelity. However, in equal-power multiple-access channels (MACs),
existing discrete semantic transceivers remain fundamentally
limited by how multiuser interference is handled. Since cross-user
interference is unavoidable under equal-power superposition, the
design objective is not to eliminate interference, but to organize
the multiuser signaling structure such that the resulting task-level
distortion is minimized. Existing methods, however, rely on the
final semantic loss to implicitly shape codebooks and receiver
behavior, leading to a strongly coupled optimization in which
updating one user alters the interference geometry experienced
by others. This makes training highly prone to poor local optima
and results in suboptimal multiuser signaling structures.
In this paper, we propose a unified E2E framework that
explicitly organizes discrete semantic signaling for equal-power
multiuser communication. Each user employs a normalized
learnable finite-alphabet codebook, and the system is trained
with a set of multiuser-aware optimization mechanisms that
directly target interference-induced distortion. In particular, we
introduce direction-sensitive interference suppression to steer
other users’ signaling away from task-sensitive directions, jointdistribution control to regulate the occurrence of multiuser symbol tuples, and sum-set geometry optimization to enlarge decision
margins for high-probability superposition patterns. To further
mitigate optimization difficulties caused by cross-user coupling,
we incorporate user-private gradient projection to suppress userconflicting updates. At the receiver, we adopt one-shot joint
demixing with a Remix-based consistency constraint, which is
better matched to equal-power superposition than conventional
cancellation-based designs.
Index Terms—Semantic communications, multiuser learning,
discrete codebooks, equal-power MAC, task-oriented inference.

Representative designs, including NOMASC, do not explicitly enforce such principle. In particular, they do not

toward directions that are insensitive to the current user’s task.
Consequently, the system can only rely on the final end-task

practice it leads to a strongly coupled optimization process:
updating the codebook of one user alters the interference
geometry experienced by all other users, thereby reshaping
their distortion landscapes and preferred updates. This crossuser feedback makes the optimization highly non-convex and
prone to poor local optima, especially in equal-power settings
where all users contribute symmetrically and no user can be
cleanly separated.
Existing receiver designs are also not well matched to
this regime. Many baselines remain rooted in interferencecancellation-style processing, which is most effective when
users can be reliably ordered or progressively peeled off. Such
assumptions are much weaker in equal-power MAC, where
no stable decoding hierarchy exists and cancellation becomes
inherently less suitable. As a result, receiver-side inference
is strongly interference-limited, and estimation errors across
users are tightly coupled.
Motivated by these observations, we propose a unified E2E
framework for equal-power multiuser semantic communication
that explicitly organizes the multiuser signaling structure from
a distortion-aware perspective. Instead of relying solely on the
final task loss to implicitly shape the system, we explicitly

with K ≥ 2 users. We consider representative semantic tasks,
including semantic reconstruction measured by PSNR and
task-oriented inference measured by top-1 accuracy, over a
wide range of SNRs and bandwidth ratios.
Notation for M -QAM. In this paper, “16QAM” and
“64QAM” refer only to the alphabet size (M = 16 or
M = 64) of the discrete semantic interface, rather than to

a fixed standards-compliant square-QAM with conventional
Gray mapping and demapping. Specifically, the constellation is
initialized from a standard QAM grid for optimization stability
and is subsequently learned end-to-end under zero-mean and
unit-average-power normalization. As a result, the final learned
constellation is not restricted to a regular square geometry
and may become irregular. Unless otherwise stated, “QAM”
denotes the case of M = 64.
Power normalization and reporting convention. For the
K-user equal-power MAC, a fixed total transmit power budget is imposed and uniformly allocated across users, i.e.,
pk = 1/K for p
each user k. Accordingly, user k contributes
a coefficient of 1/K to the superposed transmit signal. For
per-user PSNR and accuracy curves, the decoded streams are
reported using a consistent performance-based ordering, with
the best-performing stream denoted as user1, the second-best
as user2, and so on. This convention improves the readability
of the plots by reducing unnecessary curve crossings, while
preserving the relative performance trends under equal-power
superposition.

direction-sensitive interference suppression and joint distribu-

IV. C ONCLUSION AND P ROSPECT
In this work, we proposed a unified end-to-end discrete semantic communication framework that can handle both singleuser transmission and equal-power multiuser superposition
over the MAC. The design is motivated by two practical

challenges: the high-SNR performance gap of finite-alphabet
semantic transceivers and the brittleness of order-dependent
SIC under equal-power collisions. By jointly learning discrete
signaling (with learnable constellation geometry and proper
normalization) and a receiver-side one-shot joint demixing
module, the proposed system performs multiuser separation
in a single forward pass without relying on a stable decoding
order. To further stabilize the separation and enforce physical
plausibility under superposition, we incorporate a closedloop Remix constraint that re-superposes separated streams to
match the received mixture, together with per-user supervision
to mitigate permutation/entanglement ambiguities.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves strong and robust performance across a wide
range of SNRs and bandwidth constraints. In the singleuser setting, the discrete design approaches the analog upper
envelope at high SNR and consistently outperforms representative digital baselines. In the equal-power multiuser setting,
one-shot demixing combined with Remix consistency yields
graceful degradation in low SNR and effective interference
mitigation in medium-to-high SNR, avoiding cliff effects and
error propagation that commonly arise in SIC-based pipelines.
Moreover, scaling experiments with a larger number of active
users confirm that the system remains functional and stable
under heavier superposition: reconstruction quality degrades
moderately while task accuracy remains close, indicating robust semantic preservation even when interference increases.
Several directions can further improve scalability and deployment readiness. A natural extension is to move beyond
equal-power assumptions and incorporate adaptive power control and heterogeneous user objectives, enabling flexible tradeoffs between fairness and throughput under varying channel
conditions. In larger-scale grant-free settings, combining the
current architecture with explicit activity sparsity, user selection, and more structured multiple-access constraints may
further enhance collision robustness and reduce computational cost. It is also important to extend the evaluation to
more realistic channels, including fading, frequency selectivity,
synchronization errors, and imperfect CSI, and to integrate
such uncertainties more tightly into the consistency and refinement mechanisms. Finally, bridging to practical hardware
constraints (e.g., PAPR control and quantized DAC/ADC) and
expanding to richer downstream semantics (e.g., detection,
segmentation, and multimodal tasks) are promising avenues to
broaden the applicability of semantic multiple access systems.
